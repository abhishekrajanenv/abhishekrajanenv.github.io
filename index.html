Research statement

I am an environmental and resource economist. I study how people respond to changes in water resources and environmental conditions, and what those responses mean for their well-being and for public policy.

Methods
Non-market valuation (choice experiments, travel cost models, and hedonic property models) and causal inference.
Data
Primary data from surveys and lab experiments; secondary data from household surveys and administrative records; and big data, including cellphone mobility, real estate, and satellite data.

My dissertation at the University of Florida studied invasive aquatic plant management, red tide, and lake water quality in Florida. At Texas A&M, I study how drought, falling water levels, and other extreme weather events affect outdoor recreation, and how rainwater harvesting structures affect home values.

Before my Ph.D., I spent five years at the International Water Management Institute (IWMI), working on water, food, and energy in South Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa.

Does Irrigation Access Improve Diets of Farming Households? with O. Savchenko · Agricultural and Resource Economics Review, 2026, 55(2), 268–289+

Among farming households in India, irrigation access is associated with higher consumption of milk, wheat, and sugar, but little or no increase in fruits, vegetables, pulses, eggs, and meat. Groundwater irrigation has a larger effect on the intake of nutrient-rich foods than surface water irrigation.
